A few years ago, after my first startup failed, I started writing software engineering articles on HackerNoon. 
Through that community, I became friends with the founders of a growing media platform and eventually joined the founding team when the company was still fewer than seven people.

One of our biggest challenges was emails. 
We had a large audience, but creating, formatting, and sending campaigns was slow and operationally painful. 
I aggresively took ownership of the entire process and turned it into a reliable, profitable growth channel.

To scale it further, my student and I built an internal email template generator. 
Instead of manually designing every newsletter, writers could create content in a simple structured format while the visual template remained separate. The system would automatically generate a production-ready email that could be sent to more than 70,000 subscribers.

The more I worked on the system, the more I became convinced that the entire email workflow was fundamentally broken. 
I prepared a proposal to trash the existing process and rebuild it from scratch. 
The slides below are from that proposal.

My presentation style was probably more aggressive than it should have been, 
but the core idea was simple: email creation should work more like software development—structured, repeatable, automated, and scalable.

Two months later, I was fired by my friends.

Ironically, that experience became the foundation for my next startup - LLazyEmail.

The biggest insight wasn't how to send emails.
It was realizing how much friction exists between content creation, design, approval, and delivery. 
After spending two years operating email campaigns at scale, I saw the same problems repeatedly. 
That's what led me to start building an email marketing platform designed around automation, developer workflows, and operational efficiency.

There are no hard feelings. 
I'm still friends with the founders and continue to hold shares in the company.
